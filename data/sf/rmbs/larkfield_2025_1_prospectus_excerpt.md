|---|---:|---|---:|

Credit enhancement for a class is the aggregate principal amount of all classes ranking below it, divided by the aggregate principal amount of all notes. The reserve fund is a source of liquidity for senior expenses and for interest on the Class A and Class B notes; it does not pay principal and is therefore excluded from the credit enhancement figures above.

Interest on each class accrues on its principal amount outstanding on the actual number of days in the interest period divided by 365.

## 3. Reserve fund

On the closing date the issuer establishes a reserve fund of **£6,082,667.80**, equal to 1.50% of the aggregate current balance of the portfolio at the pool cut-off date (the **Reserve Fund Required Amount**). The Reserve Fund Required Amount does not amortise.

On each interest payment date, the cash manager draws on the reserve fund to the extent that available revenue receipts are insufficient to pay items (a), (b) and (c) and interest on the Class A and Class B notes in the pre-enforcement revenue priority of payments. Amounts drawn are replenished from available revenue receipts at item (l).

## 4. Principal deficiency ledger

The cash manager maintains a principal deficiency ledger with one sub-ledger for each class of notes. On each interest payment date, the current balance of every loan that became a **Defaulted Loan** during the preceding collection period is debited to the sub-ledgers in the following order, in each case up to the principal amount outstanding of the relevant class: first the Class D sub-ledger, then the Class C sub-ledger, then the Class B sub-ledger, and finally the Class A sub-ledger.

A loan becomes a **Defaulted Loan** when six or more monthly payments are due and unpaid.

## 5. Revenue and principal receipts

**Revenue receipts** comprise interest collected on the loans, including arrears of interest and of scheduled payments recovered from borrowers, and all **Recoveries** on Defaulted Loans.

**Principal receipts** comprise scheduled repayments of principal, partial prepayments and redemptions in full.

## 6. Pre-enforcement revenue priority of payments

On each interest payment date, available revenue receipts are applied in the following order of priority, in each case only to the extent that all prior items have been paid in full:

- (a) the fees of the note trustee and security trustee (£2,000 per month);
- (b) the fee of the cash manager (£1,500 per month);
- (c) the servicing fee of 0.15% per annum of the aggregate current balance of the portfolio at the start of the collection period;
- (d) interest due on the Class A notes;
- (e) amounts to reduce the debit balance of the Class A principal deficiency sub-ledger to zero;
- (f) interest due on the Class B notes;
- (g) amounts to reduce the debit balance of the Class B principal deficiency sub-ledger to zero;
- (h) interest due on the Class C notes;
- (i) amounts to reduce the debit balance of the Class C principal deficiency sub-ledger to zero;
- (j) interest due on the Class D notes;
- (k) amounts to reduce the debit balance of the Class D principal deficiency sub-ledger to zero;
- (l) amounts to replenish the reserve fund up to the Reserve Fund Required Amount;
- (m) all remaining amounts to the holders of the residual certificates.

Amounts applied under items (e), (g), (i) and (k) are added to available principal receipts.

## 7. Pre-enforcement principal priority of payments

On each interest payment date, available principal receipts are applied sequentially:

- (a) to repay the Class A notes until they are redeemed in full;
- (b) then to repay the Class B notes until they are redeemed in full;
- (c) then to repay the Class C notes until they are redeemed in full;
- (d) then to repay the Class D notes until they are redeemed in full.

## 9. Selected eligibility criteria

Each loan, on the pool cut-off date:

1. is denominated in sterling and secured by a first-ranking charge (or, in Scotland, a first-ranking standard security) over a residential property in the United Kingdom;
2. was originated by Larkfield Building Society in the ordinary course of its business;
3. has an original loan-to-value ratio not exceeding 95%;
4. is not in arrears and has never been more than one monthly payment in arrears since origination;
5. has a final maturity date no later than 31 December 2057;
6. is owed by a borrower who is a natural person resident in the United Kingdom.

## 10. Selected risk factors

**Interest rate risk.** The loans pay fixed rates during their initial period while the notes pay a floating rate linked to SONIA. The issuer has not entered into an interest rate swap. A rise in SONIA during the fixed-rate periods reduces the excess spread available to absorb losses.

**Prepayment risk.** Borrowers frequently redeem their loans when the initial fixed-rate period ends. A high rate of redemption at reversion shortens the weighted average life of the Class A notes.

**Sequential amortisation.** Principal is paid sequentially. Junior classes receive no principal until the Class A notes are repaid in full, and their credit enhancement increases as the Class A notes amortise.
